# Consumer personal AI agents and AI companions: market and competitive landscape (state as of November 2026)

Scope note: all data points are dated inline. "Measured" means a company disclosure, court filing, or named-panel measurement (Sensor Tower, Appfigures, YipitData, Similarweb). "Estimate" means a modeled figure, an aggregator, or a self-reported claim without audit. Market-research-firm forecasts (Grand View, MarketsandMarkets, etc.) are vendor models with undisclosed scope and should be treated as directional only.

## Key question 1: How big is this really, and which segment (companion vs task agent) has proven demand?

### Takeaway
General assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, Meta AI) are the only segment with proven mass demand: roughly 1B weekly/monthly users each and ~$40B global consumer AI spend in 2026 (Menlo estimate). The companion segment is real but an order of magnitude smaller in money (roughly $150M per quarter in app-store IAP in Q1 2026, Sensor Tower) and its largest independent player, Character.ai, went bankrupt in October 2026. Standalone consumer task agents only reached consumers at scale in late 2026 (Meta Muse, OpenAI Dots, Instinct) and have no revenue track record yet.

### Inferences
- Measured money in companions (~$600M/yr app-store IAP globally, Sensor Tower Q1 2026 run-rate) is less than half of ChatGPT's mobile revenue in a single quarter. Companion demand is proven in time-spent terms but not in revenue terms.
- The "task agent" segment has strong top-of-funnel in late 2026 (Muse's 10M downloads in 40 days) but its retention and monetisation are unproven; the only revenue evidence is Instinct's self-reported transaction volume, which is GMV, not take.
- Vendor market forecasts for "AI companion" ($70B-146B by 2030) are 100x the measured app-store revenue and should not be used for planning; use Sensor Tower/Appfigures run-rates instead.
- Demand concentration is extreme: ChatGPT plus Gemini plus Meta AI account for the vast majority of consumer AI usage, and a16z notes the fewest new entrants ever in its latest ranking.

## Key question 2: Which products have real retention, and what made the habit stick?

### Takeaway
Measured retention evidence is thin, but what exists points the same way: products with memory and a relationship (Character.ai at ~120 min/day; Replika's loyal tail; Tolan) and products that start acting proactively (Muse's 7/14-day retention beating ChatGPT) retain better than pure Q&A utilities. ChatGPT itself retains on breadth of use, not emotional attachment; emotional use is rare except among a small heavy-voice cohort.

### Cited Findings
- Sensor Tower (Oct 2026): Meta Muse US users show ~30% day-7 and ~23% day-14 retention; ChatGPT, Claude and Grok averaged 19% and 16% at the same marks — [9to5Mac on Sensor Tower](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/20/sensor-tower-metas-muse-app-tops-10-million-us-downloads-in-40-days/)
- Character.ai: ~120 minutes/day per active user (bankruptcy declaration, Oct 2026) — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=4327538). Note this engagement did not translate into revenue: ~$0.44/month revenue per active user (PPC Land calculation) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/character-ai-loses-8m-a-month-despite-2-5m-in-monthly-ad-sales/)
- Replika: a third-party aggregator claims ~20% day-90 retention and that an ElevenLabs voice add-on drove +20% day-7 retention. Unverified — [aicompanionpick](https://aicompanionpick.com/replika-user-base-2026). Kuyda has said Replika's chat partner becomes "tired" after ~50 messages to encourage breaks — [MindSite News interview, Feb 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/02/09/replikas-eugenia-kuyda-doesnt-believe-in-regulation/)
- Third-party vendor blogs put day-30 retention for AI companion apps at 5-15%, attributing to Sensor Tower and others; another vendor says public day-30 figures are mostly second-hand with unstated methodology. Treat as unverified — [GetKapex](https://getkapex.ai/blog/ai-companion-retention-benchmarks); [Agnost](https://agnost.ai/blog/ai-companion-retention-benchmarks)
- Tolan describes its product as built around "repeat conversations and memory" and is now pushing to become "a practical helper for everyday life" — [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/tolan-raises-30m-ai-companion-daily-assistant)
- Teens (Common Sense, 2025): 52% of teens use AI companions at least a few times a month; motives were entertainment (a third) and curiosity (28%) — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/nearly-3-in-4-teens-have-used-ai-companions-new-national-survey-finds)
- OpenAI/MIT studies (March 2025; 40M interactions, 4,076 surveyed; RCT n=981 over 28 days; not peer-reviewed): emotional use of ChatGPT is rare overall; "emotionally expressive interactions were present in a large percentage of usage for only a small group of the heavy Advanced Voice Mode users"; heavier daily use correlated with more loneliness and dependence (correlation, not causation) — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/joint-studies-from-openai-and-mit-found-links-between-loneliness-and-chatgpt-use-193537421.html); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/advanced-voice-feature-draws-heavy-chatgpt-users-into-emotional-bonds-study-finds/)
- Menlo 2026: payers are nearly 2x more likely to use AI daily (50% vs 26%) and 5x more likely to use agents (65% vs 13%); 52% of AI users use AI more than a year ago; power users are "people who pay, often a lot, for tools that help them build, create, and get work done" (a16z) — [Menlo 2026](https://menlovc.com/?p=22260); [a16z 7th ed.](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/)
- Menlo 2026 on what is working: proactive agents (Instinct), low-stakes first use that builds trust, free tier as conversion path, distribution-driven reach (Gemini), workflow-specific tools (Wispr Flow). - Rabbit r1: ~5,000 daily active users out of ~100,000 units sold about five months after launch (2024), i.e. ~5% daily use — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/early-product-releases-could-crucial-startups-competing-ai); [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/is-there-really-a-place-in-the-market-for-ai-device-rabbit-r1)
- Omi wearable: company-reported 170K+ users (Oct 2026) and 25,000+ devices sold through July 2026; marketing claim "300,000+ professionals". Not audited — [ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/research/how-omi-grew-to-10m-arr)
- a16z: Claude's consumer trend in YipitData's panel showed slowing gross additions, rising churn, and declining daily sessions in July-August 2026 — [a16z 7th ed.](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/)

### Inferences
- Retention correlates with two things in the evidence: a persistent relationship/memory (companions) or the product doing work without being asked (Muse, Instinct). Pure reactive Q&A assistants show the lowest early retention among the measured cohort.
- Time spent is not a monetisation proxy for companions: Character.ai had the best engagement in the category and the worst unit economics (see Q7).
- Hardware-only form factors (Rabbit, Humane, Friend) showed the weakest habit formation; software subscriptions (Omi, Plaud) are where wearable companies found revenue.

### Gaps
- No Sensor Tower or Appfigures day-30+ retention comparison between companion apps and general assistants was located; all day-30 figures are second-hand.
- No retention data for Replika, Nomi, Kindroid, Pi, Perplexity Comet, or Alexa+ from primary sources.
- No published DAU/MAU ratio for any companion app.

## Key question 3: Why did well-funded personal agent attempts fail?

### Takeaway
The first wave (Pi/Inflection, Humane, Rabbit, Dot, Adept, Limitless) failed on distribution (big tech bundled assistants for free), cost (inference and hardware COGS vs flat-rate pricing), reliability (devices that could not do what demos showed), and no daily habit (5% daily use on Rabbit). Character.ai's 2026 bankruptcy adds a fifth cause: flat subscriptions cannot cover the inference cost of heavy users, and ads at $0.0033 per user-hour cannot either.

### Cited Findings
- Inflection/Pi: raised $1.3B (June 2023); in March 2024 Microsoft paid a ~$650M licence fee and hired founders Mustafa Suleyman and Karén Simonyan plus most of the team; Inflection pivoted to enterprise. Analysts cite no distribution advantage ("Meta and Google baked assistants into billions of devices for free"), weak traction vs ChatGPT, and doubt consumers would pay for emotional-support differentiation. Pi remains online in 2026, free, in maintenance mode with no current user figures — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/charts/personal-ai-agent-startups); [Section AI postmortem](https://www.sectionai.com/blog/what-happened-to-inflection-and-pi); [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/why-even-the-best-funded-ai-startups-cant-compete-with-tech-giants/); [messengerbot.app 2026 status (low-reliability)](https://messengerbot.app/tl/?p=263151)

### Inferences
- Every first-wave independent personal agent either sold to big tech below capital raised (Inflection, Humane, Adept, Limitless, Bee) or shut down (Dot). The two survivors (Rabbit, Friend) are niche hardware with no disclosed growth.
- Companions do not escape the cost problem: Character.ai's heavy users (120 min/day) are exactly the users a flat subscription cannot serve profitably. Any product that wins on engagement must either price by usage, cap heavy use, or run cheap models.
- The failure pattern for utility agents is "demo works, daily life doesn't": Rabbit (5% daily), Humane (returns > sales), Mariner and Instant Checkout (merchant/platform access withdrawn). Platform permission (Amazon blocking Muse) is the newest form of this risk.

## Key question 4: Is there a gap for an agent that is both emotionally good to talk to AND does useful daily tasks? Who is attempting it?

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- The gap exists in evidence terms: no product has published both high engagement (companion-like) and task completion revenue. ## Key question 6: What do consumers say they want and not trust (acting, paying, privacy)?

### Takeaway
Across 2026 surveys, consumers accept AI for research and planning but refuse autonomous booking and payment: only 2-8% will let an AI book travel outright, three-quarters are uncomfortable with autonomous purchase even with spending limits, and 60% would abandon an agent after one mistake. Trust is highest for bank-backed systems and lowest for independent agents; privacy is the top concern among non-users (76%).

## Key question 7: What do pricing and unit economics look like (ARPU, conversion, cost per user)?

### Takeaway
The only audited companion unit economics (Character.ai) show ~$0.44/month revenue per active user against ~$0.58/month compute, with costs at $2.50 per revenue dollar. General assistants monetise far better: 4.5% of US consumers pay for ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude (median $25/month; top 1% average $903/month), but free-tier inference runs at negative margin, which is why OpenAI, Meta and Character.ai all turned to ads in 2026.

### Inferences
- A companion with Character.ai-like engagement (2 hours/day) costs ~$0.50-0.60/user/month in compute even after aggressive optimisation; at ~1% paid conversion and ~$10/month pricing that yields ~$0.10/user/month in subscription revenue, so ads or usage caps are mandatory, and Character.ai's ad yield ($0.0033/user-hour) was still 3x short.
- Conversion to paid for general assistants in the US is ~4.5-5.5% of users; companion apps' paid share appears lower (Character.ai ~139K payers on 12-20M MAU is ~0.7-1.2%). Revenue per download is roughly 2x higher for romance/NSFW than PG companions.
- Hardware companies that survive (Omi, Plaud) do so on software subscription ARPU of tens of dollars per year, not device margin.
- India ARPU is two orders of magnitude below US; an India-first product must plan for ads, telco bundles, or very low price points (Rs 399 was already discounted to zero).
