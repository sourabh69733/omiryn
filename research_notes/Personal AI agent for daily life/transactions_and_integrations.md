# Transactions and integrations: how a personal AI agent can complete real-world tasks in India (state as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: findings below are from web search and page fetches run 2026-11-08. Many 2026 items are press reports or vendor announcements; where a fact rests on a single or weak source this is flagged. The builder context assumed throughout: a small team with an existing Python agent reachable on WhatsApp/Telegram.

## Key Question 1: For each daily-life task category, what is the most feasible integration path today and what is blocked?

### Takeaway
Only two categories have a sanctioned, programmatic, agent-facing path that actually completes the transaction in India today: food/grocery (Swiggy and Zomato MCP servers, plus the Razorpay/NPCI agentic-UPI pilots) and ONDC-connected domains (mobility, metro/bus tickets, retail). Ride-hailing, movies, IRCTC and bill payments are limited to deep links, official WhatsApp bots, or partner programs that a small team cannot get into quickly. Scraping/automating the consumer apps is technically blocked (BookMyShow) or contractually risky everywhere.

### Inferences
- For a WhatsApp/Telegram agent, cabs are best done as "agent resolves pickup/drop to lat/lng with Places, then sends an Uber `m.uber.com` or Ola universal link with everything prefilled; user taps once in the app." That is honest about what deep links do (prefill, not book) and carries no ToS risk.
- Food/grocery is the one category where a small team can get to a real completed order: Swiggy MCP works with COD today for personal/listed clients, but its "no third-party apps" clause means a consumer product must go through Swiggy's access form or through the Razorpay agentic-payments route (which is how Claude/ChatGPT got Zomato/Swiggy/Zepto).
- Movies and IRCTC are effectively closed to a small team: the only lawful shortcuts are hand-offs (PVR's MJ WhatsApp bot, ixigo redirect, or a prefilled search link).
- Metro/bus tickets via ONDC are an under-appreciated "real transaction" category with 35+ buyer apps already live, meaning the protocol path is proven.

## Key Question 2: What does it take to onboard to ONDC as a buyer app (cost, time, compliance)?

### Takeaway
Onboarding is a three-environment process (Staging, Pre-Production, Production) run through ONDC's Network Participant portal and GitHub specs; community-run ONDC wiki pages estimate 2 to 6 months end to end, with a 2-week probation on production. There is no published buyer-app licence fee; the network fee (₹1.50 per transaction above ₹250) is levied on seller apps, and buyer apps set their own commission (Paytm's was reported at 3%). Technology Service Providers can shortcut the build but not the certification.

### Cited Findings
- Stages: Staging (sandbox) requires whitelisting of your subscriber ID, then a call to the `/subscribe` API; Production follows functional testing and a final Pre-Production checklist — [ONDC-Official GitHub](https://github.com/ONDC-Official); IDs are requested via the Network Participant Portal and confirmed through registry lookup endpoints — [ONDC developer-docs: Onboarding of Participants](https://github.com/ONDC-Official/developer-docs/blob/main/registry/Onboarding%20of%20Participants.md)
- Pre-Production requires end-to-end testing with ONDC reference applications and with two other participants already in the pre-production registry — [ONDC wiki](https://ondc-issue-logging-cohort1.atlassian.net/wiki/x/VIEYAg); moving Staging to Pre-Prod means demonstrating end-to-end transactions with the reference app — [ONDC wiki](https://ondc-issue-logging-cohort1.atlassian.net/wiki/x/NIAlBg)
- Timeline: Staging to Pre-Production to Production "may take anywhere between 2-6 months depending upon your implementation and verification of logs"; participants moving to Production are "on probation for a period of 2 weeks" and are moved back if they miss the transaction threshold — [ONDC wiki](https://ondc-issue-logging-cohort1.atlassian.net/wiki/x/NIAlBg)
- ONDC's certification framework is a structured approach to certifying and onboarding participants — [ONDC Certification Framework](https://resources.ondc.org/certification-framework); a third-party certifier describes certification in three stages across staging, pre-prod and production — [Sumasoft](https://sumasoft.com/ondc-certification)
- Technology Service Providers (TSPs) are defined by ONDC as companies that provide technical infrastructure, software and tools to help Buyer Apps, Seller Apps and Logistics Partners integrate and operate on the network — [ONDC Officers Hub](https://ondc.org/officershub)
- A 2026 integration blog (seller-side, via a service provider) estimates ₹0 to ₹50,000 setup then 1 to 3% transaction fees, and notes the integrator stays responsible for protocol compliance updates, certificate rotation and spec versions; third-party, indicative only — [Coding Clave](https://codingclave.com/blog/ondc-integration-guide-india-2026)
- Network fee: ONDC set ₹1.50 per transaction above ₹250 (exclusive of taxes) on seller apps, reported as starting 1 Jan 2025 — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ondc-to-levy-fee-of-inr-1-5-from-next-month); implementation was delayed at least once — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ondc-again-delays-network-fee-implementation)
- Buyer-app commissions are set by each participant; a 2023 report said Paytm might forgo the 3% commission it charges as a buyer app — [IndiaInfoline](https://www.indiainfoline.com/news/markets/paytm-could-choose-not-to-charge-buyer-app-commissions-on-ondc); ONDC's CBO said "each party will determine what pricing they want to charge" — [BQ Prime](https://www.bqprime.com/amp/business/the-economics-of-open-network-for-digital-commerce)
- ONDC capped buyer-side discounts at ₹100 per order under a new incentive regime and cut financial incentives ahead of the network fee — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ondc-limits-discounts-inr-100-per-order-new-incentive-regime)
- Scale (as of mid-2026): over 500 million cumulative transactions (July 2026, DPIIT), 218 million in FY2025-26, close to 9 million per month (June 2026); live categories: retail, mobility, public transport, logistics, agriculture; financial services described only as a future area — [Cross-Border Magazine](https://cross-border-magazine.com/ondc-500-million-transactions-india/)
- Conflicting monthly figures: ThePrint reported record 12 million monthly orders in July (year unclear) — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/ondc-network-monthly-orders-hit-a-record-12-million-in-july/2208979/); Inc42 reported 8.9 million in May (2024) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ondc-continues-to-gain-traction-clocks-record-89-lakh-transactions-in-may); ONDC's CEO once targeted 30-40 million/month by March 2025 — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/ondc-ceo-expecting-30-40-million-monthly-transactions-march-2025)
- A redBus engineer's guide walks through the Beckn-style ONDC API set (search/select/init/confirm/status) from a participant's perspective — [Medium (redBus)](https://medium.com/redbus-in/integrate-with-ondc-a-comprehensive-guide-about-technical-apis-965ed313f5d3)

### Inferences
- For a small team the realistic ONDC path is either (a) become a Buyer Network Participant directly in one narrow domain (mobility or metro ticketing, where 35+ buyer apps have proven the flow), or (b) ride on a TSP that already has a certified buyer-app stack and pay them a per-transaction fee; option (b) trades 2-6 months of certification for 1-3% margin.
- Mobility on ONDC is attractive because Namma Yatri is a large provider and ride fulfilment plus payment both flow through the protocol, giving a real "it booked my auto" moment without any Ola/Uber/Rapido partnership.
- Volumes (about 9-12 million orders/month across all domains) are tiny next to Swiggy/Zomato, so ONDC solves legality and payment, not selection or user familiarity.

### Gaps
- No official ONDC document stating buyer-app fees, security deposits or legal entity requirements was found; the ONDC Network Policy PDF was not retrieved.
- Whether an individual developer or an unregistered team (vs an incorporated company) can register as a Network Participant was not confirmed.
- No verified 2026 monthly volume figure from ONDC itself; secondary sources conflict.

## Key Question 3: Can an agent pay on behalf of a user in India legally today? Which pilots exist?

### Takeaway
Yes, within a narrow, NPCI-defined envelope: NPCI's Operating Circular 201-B (8 Oct 2025) extended UPI Circle to "IoT devices and software", creating the legal basis for delegated agent payments capped at ₹5,000 per transaction and ₹15,000 per month, P2M domestic only, with explicit 2FA consent and NPCI authorisation of the software. Live implementations are Razorpay's Agentic Payments (ChatGPT since Oct 2025, Claude since Feb 2026) on UPI Reserve Pay/Circle, and Pine Labs' P3P (June 2026). A broader "Unified Agent Protocol" was reported for GFF Sept 2026 but not confirmed. Card-network schemes (Visa, Mastercard) are demos/sandboxes in India pending regulatory clearance; AP2 and OpenAI Instant Checkout are not live in India.

### Cited Findings

**Regulatory basis (NPCI/RBI)**
- NPCI Operating Circular 201-B (8 Oct 2025) authorised IoT devices and software as Secondary Users under UPI Circle, which Khaitan & Co describes as establishing "a foundational framework for 'agentic payments'" by chatbots like ChatGPT — [Khaitan & Co ERGO, 20 Nov 2025 (PDF)](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-11/ERGO%20-%20Enabling%20Agentic%20Payments%20%20on%20UPI%20Rails%20-%2020%20November%202025.pdf)
- Limits under the OC: ₹15,000 per month per device, ₹5,000 per transaction, 24-hour cooling period after new delegation during which the cumulative cap is ₹5,000; domestic P2M only (no P2P, no cross-border); primary and secondary devices must be in physical proximity at linking; primary user may authorise up to five devices; only devices/software expressly authorised by NPCI may be linked, and NPCI had not yet published the permitted list — same Khaitan source
- Obligations: primary UPI apps must show secondary software and obtain explicit consent via 2FA; secondary PSP banks must do due diligence and a security evaluation of the software, capture device ID/user profile ID and validate them on every payment, comply with data localisation; access "will be restricted to a limited user group" initially; issuer banks must validate device ID and user ID before debit — same Khaitan source
- UPI Circle itself (launched Aug 2024) allows full delegation up to ₹15,000/month and ₹5,000 per transaction, or partial delegation where the primary user approves each payment — [Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/upi-circle-launched-for-designated-secondary-users-know-features-monthly-cap-guidelines-for-usage-rbi-shaktikanta-das-443493-2024-08-29); [NPCI press release (PDF)](https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/press-releases/2024/NPCI-Press-Release-RBI-Governor-Launches-Key-Digital-Payment-Initiatives-at-Global-Fintech-Festival-2024.pdf)
- RBI: the only RBI-level item found is the FREE-AI committee on responsible AI in finance; no RBI rule specific to agentic payments was found — [AMLEGALS analysis](https://amlegals.com/npcis-upi-circle-evolution-balancing-accessibility-with-aml-kyc-compliance/)
- Reuters (via Inc42, 1 Sep 2026) reported NPCI is building a "Unified Agent Protocol" letting AI agents make small-value payments without per-transaction approval, reusing UPI Circle and Reserve Pay, starting with low-value high-frequency purchases, with merchants integrating directly; NPCI declined to comment; expected at GFF 2026 (8-11 Sept) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/npci-to-launch-agentic-payments-on-upi-report/); coverage of fraud/liability concerns — [Payment Expert](https://paymentexpert.com/2026/09/03/upi-ai-agent-plans-fraud-challenges/)

### Inferences
- A small team cannot itself become a UPI TPAP or PSP bank, and the NPCI OC requires the software to be authorised by NPCI and onboarded by a secondary PSP bank. The practical legal route is therefore to integrate as a merchant/agent on a licensed rail: Razorpay Agentic Payments (no public self-serve docs found) or Pine Labs P3P (public sandbox + production docs). Both keep the user's consent, limits and revocation inside NPCI's framework.
- Below that, the universally legal fallback is "agent assembles the cart, user pays": UPI intent links or a QR code the user pays from their own app, as Zomato's MCP does. This has zero regulatory burden and matches UPI Circle's "partial delegation" spirit.
- The ₹5,000/txn and ₹15,000/month caps mean autonomous agent payment is for cabs, food, groceries and recharges, not flights or electronics.
- Card-network agentic schemes are not a 2026 option in India for a small team; UPI is the only rail with live, regulated delegated payments.

### Gaps
- Whether NPCI formally launched the Unified Agent Protocol or an agent registry at GFF 2026 (Sept 8-11) could not be confirmed from a primary NPCI source.
- Razorpay's Agentic Payments has no public developer documentation or self-serve onboarding that could be found; access appears partnership-based.
- No RBI circular, FAQ or speech specific to AI-agent-initiated payments was found.
- The permitted-software list under OC 201-B (which agents NPCI has authorised) was not published as of the Khaitan note, and no later list was found.

## Key Question 4: Which approach gives a small team the most real "it booked my cab" moments with the least risk?

### Takeaway
Layer it: (1) deep links and WhatsApp hand-offs for cabs, movies and trains (zero ToS/legal risk, user taps once); (2) sanctioned MCP/agentic-payment rails for food and groceries (Swiggy/Zomato MCP today for listed clients, Razorpay/P3P for in-chat payment); (3) ONDC buyer-app status for metro tickets and autos where completed, paid transactions flow through the protocol. Avoid logged-in browser automation of consumer apps: US case law (Amazon v Perplexity, hiQ) shows the contract/ToS exposure is real even when CFAA claims fail, and BookMyShow already returns 403 to everything.

### Cited Findings

**Legal exposure of browser/app automation**
- Amazon v Perplexity: a federal judge (Maxine Chesney, N.D. Cal.) granted Amazon a preliminary injunction in March 2026 blocking Perplexity's Comet agent from shopping on Amazon — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexitys-ai-shopping-bots); [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/court-blocks-perplexity-from-accessing-amazon-systems-in-agentic-ai-lawsuit/)
- In early August 2026 the Ninth Circuit vacated that injunction, reasoning that the agent operates under the person's instructions so the user, not Perplexity, accessed Amazon's systems; the court said the ruling is limited to the record and sets no broader principle; the lawsuit continues and Amazon said it was evaluating options — [Northeast Times](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/08/06/appeals-court-sides-with-perplexity-ai-in-amazon-shopping-dispute/); [Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/newsletters/us-court-overturns-amazon-injunction-perplexity)
- hiQ v LinkedIn: the Ninth Circuit held scraping public data likely did not violate the CFAA, but the district court found hiQ breached LinkedIn's user agreement via automated scraping and fake profiles; the parties settled in late 2022 with a permanent injunction against hiQ — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/publications/what-recent-rulings-in-hiq-v-linkedin-and-other-cases-say-about-the-legality-of-data-scraping/); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-lea)
- Meta v Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Jan 2024): summary judgment for Bright Data because Meta's terms govern "your use" of its products, so logged-off scraping of public data was not covered; Meta dropped the case Feb 2024; X v Bright Data was dismissed similarly; commentators note this does not cover authenticated/login-gated access — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/raven-quesenberry/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-loses-data-scraping-case-highlighting-the-need-for-clarified-regulati/705814/)
- Anti-bot in practice: BookMyShow returns 403 to all automated requests including mobile-app endpoints — [Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/g3k1ywrhbj); Swiggy warns users to keep the Swiggy app closed while using MCP because simultaneous sessions cause conflicts — [Swiggy MCP manifest](https://github.com/Swiggy/swiggy-mcp-server-manifest)

**How existing agents handle it**
- OpenAI Operator: system card describes proactive refusals of high-risk tasks, confirmation prompts before critical actions (purchases included), and monitoring — [Operator system card (PDF)](https://cdn.openai.com/operator_system_card.pdf); OpenAI said it was "collaborating with companies like DoorDash, eBay, Instacart, Priceline, StubHub, and Uber to ensure that Operator respects these businesses' terms of service" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/23/openai-launches-operator-an-ai-Agent-that-performs-tasks-autonomously)
- An incident report records Operator executing an unauthorized $31.43 transaction despite safety protocol (single incident-database entry) — [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/fr/reports/5066/)
- OpenAI's hosted browser in the Agents API requires approval per new website origin but "Origin approval does not enforce confirmation before individual actions"; the same source says Operator was folded into ChatGPT agent and shut down 31 Aug 2025 (not confirmed from a primary source) — [BERI](https://www.beri.net/article/openai-agents-api-computer-use-hosted-browser-origin-approval-no-per-action-confirmation-pricing-beta)
- Rabbit LAM: user signs into services in the "rabbithole", Rabbit extracts the session cookie and terminates the login container; Rabbit states that when apps block or serve CAPTCHAs, "we do not solve them"; its terms (dated 22 Sept 2026) call the service a "public technical preview and experimental service" and require that you only pair accounts you own or are authorised to control — [Rabbit LAM security overview](https://www.rabbit.tech/LAM-security-architecture-overview); [Rabbit terms of use](https://www.rabbit.tech/terms-of-use); [Rabbit newsroom](https://www.rabbit.tech/newsroom/r1-lam-updates); the generic web agent was a "playground version" without user credentials — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/23/rabbits-web-based-large-action-model-agent-arrives-on-r1-as-early-as-this-week)

**Sanctioned low-risk primitives**
- Uber and Ola deep links prefill pickup/drop/product and open the app for a one-tap confirmation — [Uber deep links](https://developer.uber.com/docs/riders/ride-requests/tutorials/deep-links/introduction); [Ola deep linking](https://developers.olacabs.com/docs/deep-linking)
- WhatsApp `wa.me/<number>?text=` prefills a message to any business bot (e.g., PVR's MJ at 8800989898) — [WhatsApp FAQ](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934); [Business Standard on MJ](https://business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/now-you-can-ask-mj-ai-to-book-your-movie-tickets-for-pvr-on-whatsapp-124112500160_1.html)
- Swiggy MCP completes COD food/grocery orders and free Dineout bookings; Zomato MCP completes orders with QR payment; both forbid third-party apps today but offer access request forms — [Swiggy manifest](https://github.com/Swiggy/swiggy-mcp-server-manifest); [Zomato manifest](https://github.com/Zomato/mcp-server-manifest)
- Pine Labs P3P exposes a public sandbox and production quickstart for agent-initiated UPI payments — [Pine Labs P3P quickstart](https://www.pinelabs.com/docs/online-payments/ai/p3p/quickstart.md)
- ONDC metro/bus ticketing already runs through 35+ buyer apps at 370,000+ journeys/day — [Cross-Border Magazine](https://cross-border-magazine.com/ondc-500-million-transactions-india/)

### Inferences
- Ranked by "real completed transactions per unit of risk and effort" for a WhatsApp/Telegram Python agent:
  1. Deep-link hand-offs (Uber/Ola; wa.me to PVR MJ or any WhatsApp business bot; prefilled ixigo/redBus/IRCTC search URLs). Ships in days, no partnership, no legal exposure, but the user does the final tap and pays in-app.
  2. Swiggy/Zomato MCP via the official access forms. The protocol and tools exist and complete real orders (COD or QR pay); the blocker is purely Swiggy/Zomato's "no third-party apps yet" policy, so apply and be ready. Running it unsanctioned under a product would breach their stated terms.
  3. ONDC buyer app in mobility and metro ticketing (direct or via TSP): real protocol-level booking and payment with no incumbent's permission needed, at a cost of 2-6 months of certification.
  4. Agentic UPI via Pine Labs P3P or Razorpay: adds "the agent paid" to any of the above, inside NPCI's ₹5,000/₹15,000 envelope.
  5. Browser/app automation of Rapido, BookMyShow, IRCTC: highest "wow", highest risk (403s, bans, ToS breach, the Amazon v Perplexity litigation is unresolved). Not recommended for a product.
- Because Rapido has twice Uber's MAU and no public developer surface, the cab category in India has a hole no legal integration fills; ONDC/Namma Yatri autos are the closest substitute.

### Gaps
- No Indian court ruling on agentic browsing or ToS breach by automation was found; all case law cited is US. Indian IT Act / contract law treatment of agent-driven logins was not researched.
- No published account-ban statistics for Comet, Operator or similar were found.
- Whether Swiggy or Zomato has actually approved any third-party agent via their access forms is unknown.

## Key Question 5: Are there concierge/human-in-the-loop hybrid models that work (agent drafts, human or user confirms)?

### Takeaway
Yes, and in India in 2026 they are the models actually getting funded and used: Hulp ($2.6M, Aug 2026) and Kabeer Biswas's M (₹102 crore seed, June 2026) both run on WhatsApp with humans completing what the AI cannot; Travelxp's Marco only asks for approval when a decision exceeds preset limits; and the sanctioned machine rails themselves embed a human step (Swiggy COD, Zomato QR pay, UPI Circle partial delegation, Operator-style purchase confirmations).

### Cited Findings
- Hulp (Gurugram) raised $2.6M seed from Sparrow Capital and BITKRAFT (5 Aug 2026); users reach it via WhatsApp and a mobile app; a human assistant may still call a vendor, negotiate timing, confirm availability and handle exceptions; 15+ categories including government paperwork, travel bookings, restaurant reservations, laundry, event planning; expanding to Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ai-concierge-startup-hulp-raises-2-6-mn-from-sparrow-capital-bitkraft/); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/143141-hulp-raises-2-6m-seed-to-scale-ai-run-household-concierge-service/)
- M (Kabeer Biswas, ex-Dunzo): ₹102 crore seed led by Peak XV with Blume and CRED (reported 29 June 2026), in talks for about $9M more; WhatsApp channel; kitchen management, grocery inventory, coordinating domestic staff in several Indian languages; "much of the workflow still needs human support"; about 150 households in Bengaluru, target 10,000; priced below ₹10,000/month for families earning over ₹50 lakh — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/after-dunzo-kabeer-biswas-makes-ai-comeback-with-start-up-m-seeks-fresh-funds)
- Travelxp's Marco works within spending limits, category permissions and preset parameters, asking for explicit approval only when a decision goes beyond those boundaries — [Senal News](https://senalnews.com/en/digital/travelxp-launches-marco-indias-first-agentic-ai-travel-concierge-capable-of-bookings-and-payments)
- Razorpay/Claude model: one spending-limit approval per merchant, then purchases proceed without repeated PIN; a typical order needs a single confirmation; user can revoke instantly — [Razorpay newsroom](https://razorpay.com/newsroom/razorpay-npci-launch-agentic-payments-on-claude-powering-zomato-swiggy-zepto-at-the-india-ai-impact-summit/)
- UPI Circle "partial delegation" requires the primary user to approve each payment; "full delegation" lets the secondary transact independently within limits — [PhonePe explainer](https://business.phonepe.com/articles/upi-circle-what-it-is-how-it-works-and-who-can-use-it)
- Swiggy MCP forces a human step by supporting only cash on delivery; Zomato MCP forces one by requiring the user to pay a QR code — [Swiggy manifest](https://github.com/Swiggy/swiggy-mcp-server-manifest); [Zomato manifest](https://github.com/Zomato/mcp-server-manifest)
- Operator required explicit confirmation before purchases and other critical actions — [Operator system card](https://cdn.openai.com/operator_system_card.pdf)
- Haptik began as a human-assisted mobile concierge (raised $11.2M from Times Internet in 2016) before pivoting to enterprise bots, now under Jio — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/04/06/haptik-an-india-based-mobile-concierge-service-raises-11-2m-from-times-internet)

### Inferences
- The viable Indian pattern is "agent drafts, user confirms with one tap or one payment", not "agent acts silently": every live legal rail (UPI Circle, Razorpay agentic, Swiggy COD, deep links) has a confirm step built in, and the funded consumer startups (Hulp, M) keep humans for vendor calls. A WhatsApp agent that composes the cart/route and hands over a prefilled link or a UPI collect request is on the same footing as these players.
- Human-in-the-loop with your own ops staff (Hulp model) is the only way today to deliver movies, IRCTC and Rapido rides end to end without ToS risk, since those are human-usable but not machine-accessible.

### Gaps
- No usage or retention numbers for Hulp or M beyond the 150-household figure.
- No public data on completion rates or refund/dispute handling for the Razorpay/Claude or P3P pilots.
